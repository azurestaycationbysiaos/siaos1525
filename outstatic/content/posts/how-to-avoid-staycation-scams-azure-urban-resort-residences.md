## Be Careful With Social Media Bookings

Facebook, Instagram, and TikTok can be useful places to discover staycation units, but finding a listing on social media does not automatically mean that the person behind it is legitimate.

The Department of Tourism and Philippine government agencies have warned about [fake social-media accounts](https://pia.gov.ph/news/visayas/how-to-spot-and-avoid-tourism-scams-what-to-do-as-a-victim/) impersonating legitimate hotels, resorts, and tourism businesses. Some scammers use attractive promotions, copied branding, fabricated reviews, and urgency to convince people to send payment before properly checking the booking.

Before paying for an Azure Urban Resort Residences staycation, check the host's page, history, reviews, contact information, booking process, and payment details. [The Department of Tourism has also encouraged travelers to verify reservations](https://pia.gov.ph/news/dot-7-anti-scam-drive-targets-fake-booking-accounts-preying-on-tourists/) and communicate through legitimate booking channels.

## 1. Don't Book Just Because the Price Is Cheap

An unusually low price can be tempting, especially when you're trying to find a last-minute staycation. But don't let a low price be the only reason you choose a listing.

Compare several similar Azure Urban Resort Residences units and look at what is actually included. Check the unit, amenities, guest capacity, policies, and additional fees.

[A cheap booking](https://pia.gov.ph/news/tourists-warned-vs-online-booking-scams-as-panagbenga-2026-opens/) that turns into a last-minute problem can end up costing much more.

## 2. Check the Host's History, Not Just the Number of Followers

A Facebook or TikTok page having thousands of followers does not automatically mean that the booking is legitimate.

Look at the page's history.

- How long the page has been active
- Whether it has older posts
- Whether the posts are consistent
- Whether guests are leaving genuine comments
- Whether reviews appear to come from real customers
- Whether the contact information is consistent
- Whether the photos appear to be original or copied

A social-media verification badge or large follower count should be treated as only one signal, not proof that a booking is legitimate.

## 3. Search the Business Name Before Paying

Before sending money, [search the host or business name on Google, Facebook, Reddit, TikTok, and other platforms](https://blog.gridinsoft.com/vacation-related-scams/).

Try searches such as:

- "[business name] reviews"
- "[business name] scam"
- "[business name] Azure staycation"
- "[business name] complaints"

You can also search the phone number or account name associated with the booking.

Don't automatically believe every negative post you find. Look for patterns. One angry review is different from multiple unrelated guests describing the same problem.

## 4. Ask for the Actual Booking Process

A legitimate host should be able to explain what happens after you pay.

- How will my reservation be confirmed?
- What information will I receive before check-in?
- How will I get the required access or authorization?
- Who should I contact on the day of arrival?
- What happens if I cannot reach the host?
- What is the cancellation or refund policy?

This is particularly important for properties where guests need authorization, registration, QR codes, or other access requirements.

## 5. Don't Rely on a Screenshot as Proof of Payment

Keep your actual payment confirmation and transaction reference.

A screenshot sent through chat is not the same thing as an official transaction record. Likewise, if someone sends you a screenshot claiming that a payment, refund, or transfer has been made, verify the transaction through your own banking or e-wallet application.

## 6. Be Careful When You're Pressured to Pay Immediately

One common warning sign is urgency.

- "Someone else is about to book it."
- "You need to pay now to lock the date."
- "The price will increase if you don't pay today."

Sometimes a legitimate host may genuinely have another inquiry. The problem is when you're being pressured to pay before you have enough time to ask questions or verify the booking.

If you need a few minutes to check the host, confirm the details, and understand what you're paying for, you should be able to do that.

The Cybercrime Investigation and Coordinating Center (CICC) has also warned the public about [staycation scams](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/2194158/fwd-pnp-warns-vs-vacation-scams-ahead-of-2026-summer-season), including offers with unusually low prices, pressure to pay quickly, and sellers who cannot provide proof that they actually control the accommodation.

## 7. Understand What Is Included in the Price

Don't assume that the amount advertised online covers everything.

Ask specifically about:

- Unit rental
- Pool access
- Parking
- Security deposit
- Additional guests
- Extra charges
- Celebration setups
- Check-in requirements
- Other property or community fees

Azure Urban Resort Residences pool access is not included in the unit rental fee. Guests who want to use the wave pool pay an additional ₱250 per person, per shift.

There are two pool access shifts: 7:00 AM–12:00 NN and 2:00 PM–7:00 PM. The wave pool also has scheduled wave times, and the pool is closed every Tuesday for maintenance.

Ask your host about the current access requirements, pool schedule, fees, and rules before your stay.

## 8. Keep All Your Booking Records

After making a reservation, keep screenshots or copies of:

- Your conversation with the host
- Booking confirmation
- Payment receipt
- Transaction reference
- Unit information
- Check-in instructions
- Host contact information
- Cancellation and refund terms

Don't delete the conversation immediately after paying. If something goes wrong, these records can become important when communicating with the platform, payment provider, property management, or appropriate authorities.

## 9. Don't Assume Social-Media Verification Means Everything Is Safe

A verified-looking page, large follower count, or professionally designed content should not be your only basis for trusting a booking.

Think of social media as the place where you found the booking, not necessarily the proof that the booking is legitimate.

## 10. If Something Doesn't Feel Right, Stop Before Paying

You don't have to complete a booking just because you've already spent time talking with the seller.

If the host refuses to answer basic questions, keeps changing the price, gives inconsistent information, pressures you to pay immediately, or cannot clearly explain how you'll access the property, take a step back.

It is better to lose a promotional rate than to lose the entire amount you paid.
